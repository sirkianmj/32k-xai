Executive summary

32k-XAI is a research platform that simulates a society of 32,000 autonomous cognitive agents. Each agent has a personality, a mood, a learning rate, and a fairness parameter — and every one of those numbers is drawn from real human psychometric data, not invented. At every tick, agents pair up, play an ultimatum game and a trust game, update their internal values, and accumulate wealth. Over hundreds of ticks, those individual decisions produce population-level patterns: cooperation, rejection of unfair offers, trust, and inequality.

The platform does something that neural-network simulations cannot. Every decision — who to interact with, what offer to make, whether to accept, how much to return — can be traced back to the specific named parameters that produced it, with documented uncertainty and provenance. There are no neural networks. There are no large language models. Every agent's "brain" is a small, readable set of mathematical functions.

The platform reproduces five published human benchmarks within one standard error: ultimatum offer rate, ultimatum rejection rate, trust-game send rate, trust-game return rate, and wealth Gini. That means it behaves like real human populations on well-established economic-game measures.

For engineers

A JAX-vectorised multi-agent environment. 32,000 agents run end to end in 2.9 seconds on macOS Metal, 3.7 seconds on CPU, 0.26 seconds on a modest NVIDIA GTX 1660 Ti. Deterministic given seed, N, T, and shocks. FastAPI backend, single-page HTML frontend, zero external JavaScript dependencies.

For psychologists

Population statistics drawn from IPIP-FFM (N=603k), SD3 (N=17k), DASS-21 (N=40k), and behavioural tasks. Bifactor CFA structures for Dark Triad and DASS, replicated in large samples. Every trait carries a provenance badge that tells you how much weight it deserves.

For everyone else

You can run experiments on a simulated population without writing code. Click a scenario, see the results, and look inside any single decision to see exactly what the agent was thinking. Generate reports. Export data. Everything is documented in the app's Guide tab.

What the platform is not. It does not claim to be a 100% transparent or strictly deterministic simulation of any real population. It is within-agent deterministic and between-agent stochastic, parameterised by a fixed seed. Every mechanistic trace carries documented uncertainty, reliability flags, and provenance annotations. Claims are uncertainty-aware by design.

What it is, in plain language

Imagine you could build a small town of 32,000 people and watch what happens when they play money games with each other for a few hundred days. Some people are agreeable, some are suspicious, some are stressed, some are impulsive. Each one decides who to talk to, whether to engage, how much money to offer in a split, whether to accept what's offered, and how much to give back.

That is what 32k-XAI does. Each of the 32,000 agents is a character with a personality profile drawn from real human data. At each tick, agents choose a partner from their social network, decide whether to engage, play an ultimatum game and a trust game in sequence, and update their expectations about the world. Over hundreds of ticks, patterns emerge: cooperation, mistrust, wealth inequality, retaliatory behaviour.

The difference between this and every other simulation platform is what you can ask it. You can pause the simulation, pick one agent, pick one moment, and see the full arithmetic behind its decision: the value it assigned to each option, the weight it gave to habit versus planning, the fairness penalty it applied, the reward it expected, the reward it got. Nothing is hidden.

You can then ask why that arithmetic produced that behaviour. And you can run the simulation under different conditions — resource scarcity, psychological distress, social isolation, wealth redistribution — and see exactly which benchmarks move, by how much, and through which mechanism.

What you can do with it in five minutes

  1. Open the app. Click Scenarios in the sidebar.
  2. Pick Baseline. The defaults (8,000 agents, 250 ticks, seed 42) are fine for a first run. Click Run simulation.
  3. After a few seconds, you see a benchmark chart and five result cards. Green = the simulated agents matched real human behaviour. Red = they did not.
  4. Click Trace this run's agents →. Pick an agent, pick a tick, click Show me the trace. You are now looking at the 56 individual numbers that produced one decision.
  5. Go back to Crowd. Click a trait chip. See where it came from (grounded in a human sample, or a proxy, or a synthetic prior). Filter to a subgroup and see the effect sizes.

Pitch for engineers

You are looking at a 32,000-agent reinforcement-learning environment that runs end to end in under three seconds on consumer hardware, with no neural networks anywhere in the loop. It is built on JAX, uses vmap and lax.scan to avoid per-agent Python overhead, and compiles to a single XLA kernel per tick. Every agent is a row in a global tensor; every action is a batched softmax over that tensor.

Determinism and reproducibility

The simulation is deterministic given a tuple of (seed, N, T, shocks). Two runs with the same tuple produce bit-identical traces, benchmark outputs, and wealth trajectories. The run_id is a SHA-256 hash of that tuple truncated to 16 hex characters; two runs with the same run_id are guaranteed to produce the same numbers. The canonical trace hash for the calibrated engine is 905da509bf037d50e2565b807abc6b838bc61d64c439841cede337f0f312eaea.

No black boxes

  • No DNNs. No PyTorch, no TensorFlow, no Keras in the decision loop.
  • No LLMs. Not at inference, not at training, nowhere.
  • Every parameter is named. The decision loop reads 9 calibrated scalars and 17 agent-level traits, all named and documented.
  • Every decision is a formula. Softmax over Q-values. Fehr-Schmidt utility. TD update rule. Two-step Bellman backup.

What the platform measures and reports

  • Subgroup vs rest analysis. Cohen's d, 95% CI on the mean difference, and the honest observation that at N=32,000 the p-values carry no decision-relevant information.
  • Joint distribution scatter. Pearson r with a 95% CI (Fisher z) and Spearman ρ for any two traits, on the full 32,000-agent column.
  • Bifactor CFA panels. Model comparison tables (3-correlated / unidimensional / bifactor) with per-item standardised loadings on the general and specific factors.
  • APA-ready paragraphs. Methods and results sections generated from the actual data, with sample sizes, fit indices, and the exact reproducibility hash.
  • Trace view. Per-agent, per-tick arithmetic with click-through definitions for all 56 fields.

What is honestly limited

  • The DASS bifactor CFA was run on DASS-42, not the spec-mandated DASS-21. The bifactor structure replicates in both, and the general-distress construction is unaffected, but this is documented as an honest deviation from the specification.
  • Fehr-Schmidt is simplified to envy-only: α = D + λ_ineq, β is a proxy from Agreeableness. Flagged [ENVY-ONLY] in every trace.
  • α_R and α_P were collapsed into a single learning rate because the seven-parameter model failed MCMC convergence. Flagged [COLLAPSED-LR].
  • The reward scale used by the engine is not identical to the human-data scale in the ultimatum and trust games. Flagged [REWARD-SCALE-DRIFT].
  • Two parameters remain synthetic priors: [SYNTHETIC-PRIOR-PARAMS].

Pitch for everyone else

This is a simulation of a small society. It is a tool for asking what happens when people's circumstances change — when resources get scarce, when people feel stressed, when they lose their social connections, when wealth gets redistributed.

Real experiments on these questions are impossible. You cannot ethically starve a population to see what happens. You cannot take the same 32,000 people and run them twice, once with one condition and once with another, to see the difference. But you can do both of those things in a simulation, if the simulation is good enough to trust.

32k-XAI is designed to be good enough. Every agent's personality is drawn from real human data. The way agents decide what to do matches how real people play economic games, within the tolerance of the published literature. And crucially, you can look inside any single decision to see what drove it.

An example

Suppose you want to know what happens to trust in a society when people become socially isolated — fewer friends, fewer connections. The app has a scenario for that. You click it, and the app tells you: rejection rates rise, inequality rises, and the people who lose connections become less trusting, not more. It also shows you why, by letting you pick one affected agent and read every number that went into its decisions.

You can print a report of the whole experiment. You can compare two experiments side by side, controlling for everything that should be controlled. You can export the data. Everything is reproducible: the same inputs give the same outputs, always.

Scientific foundations

Three empirical pillars

1. Bifactor Dark Triad

The published DT³ article triangulated across five computational layers and found that Machiavellianism, Psychopathy, and Narcissism are best modelled as a general Dark Core plus three specific factors. Our own CFA on the SD3 (N=17,740) replicates this: the bifactor model outperforms three correlated factors by CFI +0.053, RMSEA 0.0566 vs 0.0675, and Δχ² = 9,053.7 on 24 df (p < 1e-100). The Dark Triad items do not merge into a single community when isolated, but they do when embedded in the full personality space — consistent with a Dark Core that collapses under broad covariance.

2. Bifactor DASS

Across eight countries (N=2,580), the DASS-21 fits best as a bifactor structure: one general distress factor plus three specifics. Our CFA on the DASS-42 (N=39,775) reaches the same conclusion: bifactor wins by CFI +0.034, RMSEA 0.0492 vs 0.0586, Δχ² = 36,794.9 on 39 df. This is why the platform's stress-driven modulation of the arbitration weight uses the general distress factor, not the three subscales independently.

3. Parameter recovery from behaviour

Each agent-level parameter is estimated from a behavioural task with a documented recovery metric. The recovery protocol is standard: generate synthetic subjects with known parameters, fit the model family you intend to use on real data, and report rank-order recovery (ρ), bias, RMSE, and posterior coverage.

Calibration status

The engine is calibrated against five human benchmarks (below). As of the current release, all five pass: every platform value is within one standard error of the human target. Two mitigations were required to reach this state:

  • The learning rate was collapsed from two parameters to one ([COLLAPSED-LR]), because the two-parameter model is not identifiable on the available trial counts.
  • The Fehr-Schmidt utility function was simplified to envy-only ([ENVY-ONLY]), for the same reason.

Both simplifications are documented in the source code, in the audit block of every run, and in every XAI trace.

The five human benchmarks

Every run is scored against five numbers that have been measured in real human laboratories. PASS means the platform value is within one standard error of the published human estimate.

What PASS and FAIL mean

Each benchmark is a single number from the simulation compared against a single number from the human literature, with a tolerance band equal to one standard error of the human estimate. PASS means the platform value falls inside that band. FAIL means it falls outside. Both are findings; FAIL is not an error, it is a documented difference that the platform treats as data.

Important caveat. PASS does not mean the platform is the truth about human behaviour. It means the simulation produces numbers consistent with a particular set of published studies. Replication in a different cultural or methodological context could shift any of these targets.

The population and its provenance

Every agent is a 26-dimensional vector sampled from a Vine Copula estimated on real human psychometric data. The columns are named, documented, and each carries a provenance record: where it came from, how many subjects it was estimated on, and what its status is.

Four provenance statuses are used:

  • GROUNDED — the column came from a direct empirical sample of the named construct.
  • PROXY — a defensible but weaker stand-in; the link to the target construct is documented and defended, but is not a direct measurement.
  • DERIVED — a deterministic function of other columns (e.g., the bifactor general factor).
  • SYNTHETIC — a literature-derived prior, flagged in every XAI trace.
  • INERT — present in the engine's parameter list but never read by the calibrated decision loop; retired from the copula.

What the copula is

A Vine Copula (D-Vine or R-Vine) estimated from these samples. Copulas separate the marginal distribution of each variable from the dependence structure between them, and Vines extend this to high dimensions by decomposing the joint into a cascade of pairwise copulas. This matters because human data is not Gaussian: tail dependence and asymmetry are real, and a Gaussian copula cannot represent them.

The current calibrated copula is synthetic_32k_p6d.pkl, shape (32000, 26). It was selected over the earlier P6c copula after the P6d project grounded four of the ten synthetic priors in external data and retired the four inert parameters.

Three layers

The per-tick decision loop

  1. Partner selection. Each agent samples a neighbour from its Watts-Strogatz network (k=6, p=0.1). If the partner chose back, the pair is mutual.
  2. Role assignment. A coin flip determines who proposes in the ultimatum game; the other responds. In the trust game, roles invert.
  3. Arbitration. Each agent's effective model-based weight is w_eff = clip(w_base · (1 + C · 0.3) · (1 - distress · 0.8), 0, 1). Distress suppresses planning.
  4. Stage-1 choice. Engage or avoid, by softmax over Q_net = w_eff · Q_MB + (1-w_eff) · Q_MF with engagement bias (E · 0.5 - a_inhib · 0.2) and avoidance bias (theta_T · 0.5 + u_U · 0.3).
  5. Transition. The environment returns the expected state with probability 0.7, otherwise the alternate state.
  6. Stage-2 action. One of four categories (self-max, self-mild, fair, generous) chosen by softmax over Q_s2 with Dark Triad bias terms.
  7. Ultimatum game. Proposer offers [2, 4, 5, 7] of endowment 10. Responder accepts if Fehr-Schmidt utility U = payoff - α · max(other - mine, 0) - β · max(mine - other, 0) is non-negative.
  8. Trust game. Sender invests [0, 3, 6, 9] of endowment 10; trustee returns a multiple [0, 1, 2, 3] of the amount sent.
  9. Reward aggregation. Payoffs from both games are summed, modified by omega_social · (1 + salience + 0.05 · mood), and clipped.
  10. Learning. Q tables update with the TD rule at rate alpha_R. P_hat (transition model) updates at rate eta_vol.
  11. Distress. Rises on rejection by 0.05 · (1 + N · 1.0), decays by 0.95 per tick, pulled toward the baseline by 0.05.
  12. Wealth. Accumulated reward, clamped at 0, optionally redistributed toward the population mean.

The simulation engine

What happens in one tick, visually

Every tick, for all 32,000 agents in parallel:

  [1] Partner matching
      sample neighbour  -->  mutual?  -->  assign roles (proposer / responder)

  [2] Arbitration
      w_eff = w_base * (1 + C * 0.3) * (1 - distress * 0.8)

  [3] Engage or avoid
      Q_net = w_eff * Q_MB + (1-w_eff) * Q_MF
      L1 = tau * [Q_net[engage]+bias, Q_net[avoid]+bias]
      a1 ~ softmax(L1)

  [4] Transition
      s2 = expected (70%) or unexpected (30%)

  [5] Stage-2 action (4 categories)
      a2 ~ softmax(beta_choice * (Q_s2 + dark_triad_bias))

  [6] Play ultimatum + trust
      offers   = [2, 4, 5, 7] / 10
      sends    = [0, 3, 6, 9] / 10
      returns  = [0, 1, 2, 3] multiples of sent

  [7] Rewards + learning
      reward = (payoffs from both games) * social_mod
      Q heads updated with TD rule at rate alpha_R
      P_hat updated at rate eta_vol

  [8] Distress
      distress = 0.95 * distress + 0.05 * baseline + rejection_bump

  [9] Wealth
      wealth = max(wealth + reward + redistribution_pull, 0)

Specification flags and caveats

Every XAI trace carries an active flag set. Each flag corresponds to a documented limitation. When a number from this platform appears in a publication, the flags that were active at the time of the run should be reported alongside it.

FlagMeaning
[SYNTHETIC-PRIOR]Parameter not directly estimated from human data; prior derived from literature.
[SYNTHETIC-PRIOR-PARAMS]One or more columns fall into the previous category; the current set is eta_vol and gamma_sal.
[LOW RECOVERABILITY]Parameter recovery ρ < 0.7.
[HIGH SHRINKAGE]Posterior shrinkage factor < 0.7.
[SIMPLIFIED MODEL]Parameter simplified due to identifiability constraints.
[COLLAPSED-LR]Learning rate collapsed from two parameters to one.
[KOOL-VALIDATION-PASSED]w_MB validated on the Kool paradigm in addition to Daw.
[RECOVERY-N60-PROVISIONAL]Recovery run with N=60 synthetic subjects; N=500 run required for final sign-off.
[ENVY-ONLY]Fehr-Schmidt is simplified to envy-only; guilt is a proxy from Agreeableness.
[REWARD-SCALE-DRIFT]Engine reward scale differs from the human-data scale used to fit parameters.
[COPULA-COLUMN-IS-LOG-K-DD]The k_DD column is stored as log(k), not k.
[KDE-TAIL-EXTRAPOLATION]Some tail behaviour comes from KDE extrapolation, not direct samples.
[P6D-RANK-TRANSFORM-REJECTED]A previous attempt to replace synthetic priors with rank-transforms of other columns was rejected and reverted.
[MF-GENERALIZED]Model-free updating uses a generalised update rule, not canonical TD.
[PER-HEAD-Q]Each decision head (offer, send, accept, return) has its own Q table.
[FEHR-SCHMIDT-CANONICAL]The accept rule uses the canonical 1999 QJE form.
[HUMAN-BENCHMARK-PASS-5-OF-5]All five benchmarks pass within one SE.
[NOT-IDENTIFIABLE-IN-HCP-UGR]Some traits cannot be identified from the HCP unrestricted sample; the fact is carried in every trace.

What is left

Packaging for Windows and Linux

The macOS bundle is complete and ships as a self-contained .app. The Docker image is written but predates this session's engine changes; it needs guide_content.py added to its whitelist before building. A docker-compose.yml and launcher scripts (run.sh for Linux and macOS, run.bat for Windows) are the natural next step. A native PyInstaller bundle for Windows or Linux would require building on those platforms; the Mac cannot cross-compile JAX reliably.

Glossary

TermDefinition
AgentOne simulated person. Default 8,000; full scale 32,000.
TickOne round of the simulation. Every agent picks a partner, decides whether to engage, plays both games, updates its values.
Q-valueAn agent's estimate of the reward from a given action. Higher Q means the agent expects this action to be more rewarding.
Model-free value (Q_MF)Habit. Value learned from recent experience by trial and error.
Model-based value (Q_MB)Plan. Value computed by simulating the future using a belief about state transitions.
Arbitration weight (w_mb_eff)Blend between habit and plan. Near 0 = habit, near 1 = plan. Distress reduces this weight.
SoftmaxFunction turning Q-values into action probabilities. Higher Q = more likely, with some randomness.
Inverse temperature (τ)How sharply softmax prefers the best option.
Fehr-Schmidt utilityFairness-adjusted value of accepting an offer. Subtracts penalties for disadvantageous and advantageous inequality.
DistressInternal state that rises when offers are rejected and decays slowly. High distress suppresses planning.
Gini coefficientSingle number from 0 (everyone equal) to 1 (one agent owns everything) summarising wealth inequality.
Bifactor structureA latent model with one general factor plus orthogonal specific factors. Used for Dark Triad and DASS.
Vine CopulaA decomposition of a joint distribution into a cascade of pairwise copulas, allowing non-Gaussian tail dependence.
run_idSHA-256 hash of (copula_ops, N, seed, shocks), truncated to 16 hex characters. Identifies a run.
Paired counterfactualTwo runs with the same seed and N, hence the same agents. The only difference is the intervention.
XAI traceA 56-field per-tick dump of every number behind one agent's decision.
Provenance statusOne of GROUNDED / PROXY / DERIVED / SYNTHETIC / INERT, attached to every trait.
